Early Termination of Probation in Duval County

Duval shares the Fourth Judicial Circuit with Clay and Nassau. Of the counties covered on this site it has the most crowded circuit, which makes identifying your own county the necessary first step.

Ending Duval County probation early

Jacksonville's consolidated city-county government means that in everyday speech "Jacksonville" and "Duval County" are used more or less interchangeably. For most purposes that is harmless. For working out where a motion goes, it is worth being exact, because the surrounding counties are part of the same circuit but not the same county.

Someone sentenced in Clay who now lives in Jacksonville, or sentenced in Duval who now lives in Nassau, is in a circuit that covers both — and still has a case belonging to one specific county.

The Fourth Judicial Circuit

The Fourth Judicial Circuit covers three counties: Duval, Clay and Nassau. Jacksonville is the Duval County seat.

A three-county circuit is the most spread out arrangement among the counties on this site. Sharing a circuit does not pool the cases: your matter belongs to the county where sentence was imposed, and that is where a motion to end the term is filed.

If your sentence came from Clay or Nassau rather than Duval, the circuit is the same but the county is not, and this page is not the right starting point. We have not written pages for those counties yet.

The Duval County courthouse, its criminal division structure, how the circuit distributes work across its three counties, the probation offices serving the area, and local practice on early termination motions are not published here. None of it was verifiable when this page was written.

Confirm with the Clerk of Court and the Fourth Circuit, or ask us about the division your case sits in.

Filing and hearing, in general terms

The motion is filed in the existing criminal case in the sentencing court. In a circuit covering three counties, confirming the county on your judgment before filing is not a formality — a motion directed to the wrong county costs time that is difficult to recover.

From there the sequence is the standard one: service on the State, the supervising officer's assessment, then a ruling on the papers or at a hearing. See filing a motion and the court hearing.

Eligibility considerations

The statutory factors apply the same way here as everywhere in Florida: how much of the term has been served, whether the conditions are complete, whether restitution and costs are satisfied, and whether any violation is pending. See eligibility.

Where a three-county circuit does make a practical difference is in records. Financial balances are held by the clerk of the county where sentence was imposed, so if you have moved within the circuit, that is not necessarily the clerk nearest you. Establish which office holds your file early — see documents required.

Related probation matters

If discharge is premature, administrative probation reduces reporting, and modification changes a specific condition that no longer fits.

Northeast Florida sits close to the Georgia state line, so out-of-state travel is a routine question rather than an exceptional one. See travel permission, and interstate transfer if the plan is to move rather than visit.

Nearby areas

Duval borders Clay, Nassau, St. Johns and Baker. Clay and Nassau share its circuit; St. Johns and Baker do not.

Duval is currently the only Northeast Florida county with a page here. That reflects what has been written rather than any limit on where a case can be reviewed, and it is not a claim about offices. Whether representation is available depends on the case and its location.

Duval County questions

Are "Jacksonville" and "Duval County" the same thing for my case?

For most everyday purposes yes, given the consolidated city-county government. For identifying your case, go by the county named on the judgment and sentence rather than by the city — particularly if any part of your history involves Clay or Nassau.

I was sentenced in Clay but live in Jacksonville. Does this page apply?

Only partly. You are in the same circuit, but your case is a Clay County matter and that is generally where a motion is filed. The general material on eligibility and process applies to you; the Duval-specific parts do not.

Which clerk holds my restitution balance?

Generally the clerk of the county where sentence was imposed, which within this circuit may not be the office nearest you. Get the figure in writing from the right one before filing.

I cross into Georgia regularly. Does that need permission?

Out-of-state travel almost always requires approval in advance, and proximity does not change that. Regular crossings are worth addressing as a standing arrangement rather than trip by trip — see travel permission.
