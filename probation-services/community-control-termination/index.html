Ending Community Control in Florida

Community control is not probation. It is a stricter form of supervision, administered differently, and what can be asked for is not the same. Getting that distinction right is the starting point.

On this page

  1. Community control is not probation
  2. What can be asked for
  3. When this comes up
  4. What the court may consider
  5. The general process
  6. Documents and information that help

Community control is not probation

People use "probation" loosely to mean any supervision served in the community. Under Florida law the two are distinct, and confusing them leads to advice that does not apply.

Probation is community supervision with conditions and scheduled reporting. Community control is markedly more restrictive: it involves intensive monitoring and confinement to an approved residence, with movement limited to specifically authorised activities such as work, treatment or approved errands. It is often described as a form of house arrest, and it is used as an alternative to incarceration rather than as a lighter option than probation.

Check which one your sentence actually imposed

The judgment and sentence names it. Advice written about probation — including much of the rest of this site — should not be applied to community control without checking that it holds. See types of probation.

What can be asked for

Broadly, three things: ending supervision early, converting community control to ordinary probation, or modifying a particular condition within it.

Conversion is often the realistic request. Where a court is not willing to discharge supervision, moving from community control to probation still changes daily life substantially — the confinement element is what most people are actually trying to escape.

Whether each of these is available, and on what basis, depends on the sentence and the statutory provisions that apply to it. That is a question to answer from your paperwork rather than from a general rule.

When this comes up

  • The confinement requirement conflicts with work that has changed since sentencing.
  • A significant part of the term has been served with a clean record.
  • Required programmes are complete and what remains is the restriction itself.
  • Caring responsibilities have arisen that the schedule cannot accommodate.
  • The original reason for the intensity of supervision no longer applies.

What the court may consider

  • How much of the term has been served.
  • The compliance record — with community control, that includes adherence to the schedule and any monitoring.
  • Completion of required programmes and treatment.
  • Restitution, fines, costs and supervision fees.
  • The offense, and why community control rather than probation was imposed.
  • The position of the supervising officer and the State.
  • Whether a less restrictive form of supervision is now adequate.

That last point is the heart of it. Because community control is typically imposed as an alternative to incarceration, a court is weighing whether the reasons for that choice still hold.

The general process

  1. Establish from the judgment and sentence exactly what was imposed.
  2. Confirm which statutory provisions govern it — this is where the difference from probation bites.
  3. Gather the compliance record and completion documentation.
  4. Confirm financial balances in writing with the clerk.
  5. File the appropriate motion in the sentencing court and serve the State.
  6. Attend the hearing, which is more likely to be set than for a routine probation motion.

The mechanics resemble those in the termination process, but the substance of the request differs and should not be assumed identical.

Documents and information that help

  • Judgment and sentence, and the written conditions.
  • The supervision and monitoring record.
  • Completion documents for every programme and requirement.
  • Written balances for restitution, fines, costs and fees.
  • Employment documentation, particularly where a schedule conflict is the reason for the request.

Common questions

Does the Florida 50% rule apply to community control?

Do not assume it does. Discussion of the halfway point in Florida is framed around probation, and community control is governed differently. This is exactly the kind of question to resolve from your own sentence. See the 50% rule, which is written about probation.

Is converting to probation easier than ending supervision?

It asks the court for less, which often makes it a more realistic request. It does not make it automatic, and the court still weighs the same considerations.

How are violations treated compared with probation?

Because community control is imposed as an alternative to incarceration and involves closer monitoring, an alleged violation is a serious matter. Get advice immediately — see violations.
